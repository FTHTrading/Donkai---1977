SIX CHAPTERS

Real Life · Programmed Dopamine · One Continuous Curriculum

Six vignettes covering fifty years. Written in the second person because you were there, whether you know it or not. Every chapter maps to a real enum value or contract in the Donkai stack. None of these are jokes about the tech — they are what the tech is for.

I
The Quarter
1985 · Era 1 · PhysicalArcade

You slid your father's quarter into the coin slot at Aladdin's Castle. The cabinet clunked — that unmistakable mechanical acknowledgment of a $0.25 transfer of value into a token bucket you would never own. You had ninety seconds of Galaga. When Player Two asked to join, you had to feed another quarter.

This was your first tuition in liquidity. The machine did not care about your feelings. The floor was sticky. Everything smelled like popcorn and Freon. It was the greatest room you had ever been in.

on-chain · Native gas token · Feed the machine to interact with the machine.
II
The Ticket Roll
1994 · Era 2 · PaperTicket

You had a plastic bag containing 3,847 paper Skee-Ball tickets, accumulated across four summers of birthday parties at Discovery Zone. The prize counter attendant was seventeen and did not care. She counted your tickets by weighing them on a scale.

You traded the bag for a stuffed dolphin whose left flipper detached in the car ride home. Your mother told you this was called depreciation. You did not use that word until 2019, when you understood it was also what happens to a governance token six weeks after airdrop.

on-chain · ERC-20 emissions from a staking contract · The dolphin is your governance token.
III
The Beanie
1998 · Era 2 · PaperTicket (Speculative)

Your mother bought you a Princess Diana Beanie Baby the week of the funeral. She kept the tag pristine, in a rigid plastic protector. She showed you a magazine that said it would be worth $2,000 by 2015.

In 2015 you sold it on eBay for $4.87. She insisted this was because the magazine was wrong about the year, not wrong about the mechanism. She still has the Diana bear listing saved in her browser bookmarks under "college fund." She still checks it, occasionally, in the way one checks a healed break in bad weather.

on-chain · JPEG floor price · The mechanism is always right; the year is always wrong.
IV
The Horse Armor
2006 · Era 3 · DigitalMicrotrans

The forum thread was 47 pages by morning. Bethesda had charged $2.50 for cosmetic horse armor in Oblivion. Every reply was an outraged declaration that this would kill gaming. Every reply was posted from an account that had also, silently, already purchased the armor.

Bethesda's stock did not go down. This was the moment you understood revealed preference — the gap between what people say they want and what they actually pay for. Twenty years later, protocol governance forums replicate this thread verbatim, weekly, at a resolution you can no longer distinguish from parody.

on-chain · Optional aesthetic microtransactions on functional infrastructure · The complaints and the purchases are the same signal.
V
The First Signature
2016 · Era 4 · AgenticYield (dawn)

The MetaMask extension asked you to sign a transaction. You had heard about wallets. You had not heard about gas. The gas cost was $47. The token being minted was a JPEG of a cat that looked exactly like the cat next to it, and the cat next to that one, and the cat next to that one, in a 100 × 100 grid of nearly-identical cats.

You bought one. It cost you a Saturday of research and half a week's coffee budget. The cat is still in your wallet. So is the exact hex-encoded memory of that $47 gasPrice. You have never once been able to explain, out loud, to a person who was not on the internet in 2017, why any of it mattered. It still does.

on-chain · Provably-scarce disappointment · Signed by your own private key, so the disappointment is undeniably yours.
VI
The Return
2026 · Era 4 · AgenticYield (present)

The quarter. The tickets. The beanie. The horse armor. The cat. You are still holding all of them. They are all in the same wallet now — a leather one from 1997 that also contains a Blockbuster card, a Costco card, and a stack of MetaMask seed phrases you copied down on a receipt for chinese food. Everything is a token now. Everything always was.

Donkai indexes all of it as instances of the same Era enum. Every relic gets a Merkle root and a Stubbornness Score. Your wallet did not change; the ledger caught up. You have been prepared for this system for fifty years. It has been prepared for you for six. Welcome to the museum. It has been open the whole time.

on-chain · DonkaiTimelineRegistry.mintRelic() · One Merkle root, one relic, one canonical entry per document. Everyone else's memory is now your museum.
